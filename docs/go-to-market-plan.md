# Digital Go-to-Market Plan

## Product / Prototype Name

Student Apartment Finder Platform

## Target Early Users

The first target users are university students around Rangsit University and Bangkok University who are actively searching for off-campus accommodation. Priority users include first-year students, international students, students moving from another province, and students who are unfamiliar with nearby apartment areas.

Secondary early users include students looking for roommates and landlords who want to present structured apartment information to students.

## Main Value Proposition

Students can compare approved apartment listings near their university by price, distance, room type, facilities, photos, and map location in one platform.

## Early-User Goal

The first campaign will introduce the working prototype to a small group of students, encourage them to browse apartment listings, and collect feedback about the usefulness and clarity of the platform.

## Channel Plan

| Channel | Target User | Message Angle | User Action Expected | Metric to Track | Why This Channel Fits |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| University LINE or class chat groups | Current and incoming students | Find apartment information without searching through many unrelated posts | Open the landing page and browse apartments | Landing-page views, CTA clicks, and demo attempts | Students already use class and university chats for housing questions |
| Campus QR posters | Students walking through campus, dormitory, library, and cafeteria areas | Quickly compare nearby student apartments | Scan the QR code and open the prototype | QR visits, landing-page views, and CTA clicks | The promotion appears near the users and locations connected to the housing problem |
| Instagram student-community pages | First-year, international, and socially active students | See apartment choices, photos, prices, and locations in one place | Visit the landing page and try the apartment search | Post reach, link clicks, and demo attempts | Students frequently use Instagram to discover services and university information |
| Student clubs or international-student support channels | International students and students new to the area | Find structured housing information without strong local knowledge | Browse listings and provide feedback | Demo attempts and feedback responses | These groups directly support users who may experience the greatest housing-search difficulty |

## Main User Actions

The campaign will encourage users to:

1. Open the landing page.
2. Read the problem, solution, and feature summary.
3. Click **Browse Apartments**.
4. Search or filter the available apartment records.
5. Open an apartment detail page.
6. Provide feedback about the experience.

## Launch Sequence

1. Complete the landing page and confirm that its CTA opens the working prototype.
2. Test the landing page on laptop and mobile screen sizes.
3. Share the landing-page link with a small class or university chat group.
4. Prepare a QR code linked to the same landing page.
5. Share one short Instagram or student-community message.
6. Record landing-page views, CTA clicks, demo attempts, and feedback.
7. Compare results across the selected channels.
8. Improve the headline, CTA, message, or prototype flow based on the results.

## Data Collection Method

Early campaign activity will be recorded in `data/acquisition-metrics-template.csv`. Page views and CTA clicks may be collected using a simple landing-page counter, localStorage, prototype logs, or manual testing records. Feedback should use a short form that avoids requesting passwords, identity documents, financial data, or unnecessary personal information.

## Risks and Mitigation

| Risk | Possible Effect | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Students may assume every listing is guaranteed to be available | Users may rely on outdated availability information | Display listing status and encourage users to confirm details with the landlord |
| Marketing messages may promise unsupported features | Users may expect functions that are not present in the prototype | Mention only features currently demonstrated by the prototype |
| Low CTA click rate | Students may not understand the benefit or notice the main action | Test clearer CTA wording and place the button prominently |
| Limited feedback responses | The team may not learn why users stop using the page | Keep the feedback form short and explain why feedback is useful |
| Privacy concerns | Users may avoid the prototype or submit unnecessary information | Display a responsible-data message and request only information needed for the test |
| Incorrect or unsafe QR link | Users may reach the wrong page | Test the QR code before publishing and use the official landing-page address |
| Cold-start delay on the hosted prototype | Users may leave before the prototype loads | Display a short loading message or warn testers that the first load may take a moment |

## Success Decision

The initial go-to-market test will be considered useful if the team can identify which channel produces the most landing-page visits, CTA clicks, demo attempts, and feedback responses. The results will be used to improve the message and landing-page design before Lab 13.
